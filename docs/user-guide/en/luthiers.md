# Luthier workshops and authenticity reports

Find a workshop, request an inspection and keep attributed professional reports with your instrument.

## Working hours and available slots

Workshop owners can open **My luthier workshop → Workshop availability** and set a time zone, working days, opening and closing times, and an appointment length. The same hours apply to every selected day. Save the settings and enable the calendar to offer slots to musicians.

The day calendar shows your confirmed visits and the remaining free slots. Choose a date to browse the next 90 days. Other musicians see only free times when requesting a visit, without names or details of other appointments.

Use **Blocked dates** for whole days off. A day with a confirmed appointment must have that appointment cancelled before it can be blocked. **Unblock** restores the usual working hours. You can plan blocked dates up to a year ahead.

When a calendar is enabled:

1. On the appointment request page, choose a date and select **Show available times**.
2. Pick an available slot. Its time zone and length come from the workshop; you do not need to convert the time yourself.
3. Send your message. A pending request does not hold the slot.
4. The other participant confirms the proposal. Availability is checked again at that moment; two overlapping appointments cannot both be confirmed.
5. If the slot has been taken or the workshop’s hours changed, choose another available slot. Counter-proposals use the same slot picker.

Cancellation frees the reserved time. Changing working hours or slot length keeps existing confirmed visits at their agreed time and length. A pending proposal may need a new choice after those settings change. Times that are ambiguous, missing or change length during a clock change are omitted.

Until the luthier enables the calendar, free-form time proposals remain available. They use a 30-minute duration, and confirmation still checks for overlaps. Older appointments whose duration was not recorded also use 30 minutes; if a visit needs a different length, cancel and arrange a new request with the correct calendar setting.

## Arrange an appointment before the visit

You can contact a workshop without adding an instrument first.

1. Open its public profile and choose **Request an appointment**. Sign in if needed.
2. Enter a subject and message, suggest a future date and time, and choose the workshop’s time zone, such as `Europe/Paris`, or choose a free calendar slot when the calendar is enabled.
3. Send the request and follow it in **My space → My appointments**. The workshop receives an in-app notification.
4. Exchange private replies. While the request is pending, either participant can suggest another time. Only the other participant can confirm or decline the latest proposal.
5. Check the **Confirmed** status before treating the time as agreed. Both parties can still send messages or cancel; cancellation closes the conversation and keeps its history.

Every proposal shows its time zone and remains in the history. If the selected hour occurs twice or does not exist during a clock change, choose a different hour. Reopen the appointment if the form says another update has arrived. After confirmation, arrange a change through a new request and cancel the old appointment if needed.

Appointment conversations stay private to the musician and workshop owner, including after an instrument transfer. They do not become professional reports. Notifications stay inside the app; there are no appointment emails, automatic reminders or external calendar synchronisation. An enabled availability calendar checks and reserves time at confirmation. Confirming records your agreement; agree the visit’s practical details in the conversation.

## Request work as an instrument owner

1. Browse **Discover → Luthiers** and open a workshop profile. Check the workshop’s identity-verification status, specialties and published contact details.
2. Open your instrument and choose **Request a luthier intervention**.
3. Select the workshop and type of work, describe your request, and send it.
4. Follow the request from **My space → My luthier interventions**. The assigned luthier records a report to complete the request. Either participant can cancel while it is pending.
5. Read the report, its author, date, findings, evidence and limitations. If a correction is recorded, both revisions remain available.

Your request text is private to you and the assigned workshop. It does not automatically become part of the next owner’s passport view.

## Publish a workshop and record work

From **My space → My luthier workshop**, fill in your workshop profile and choose whether to publish it in the directory. Only publish contact details you agree to share. Creating a profile does not verify your professional identity; professional identity verification is recorded by an administrator after review.

Manage assigned requests from **My luthier interventions**. Complete a pending request by saving an attributed report. Record the conclusion, inspection date, findings, evidence and limitations. To correct an existing report, add a correction with its reason; previous revisions are retained.

## Choose attachment visibility

A report attachment is **Private** by default. The author can instead share it with the original request participants, or with report readers, including future current owners. Keep invoices and other personal files private when they should not follow the report.

Professional reports follow the instrument after an accepted transfer. Avoid writing personal or financial information in report text if it should remain private. A verified workshop identity and an author’s authenticity assessment are distinct: check each report’s evidence and limitations.

Read [document access](documents.md) and [what follows a transfer](transfers.md).

## Follow a repair from intake to collection

For a physical workshop visit, add the instrument to your collection and request an intervention from the workshop. Your appointment page links to your instruments with that workshop preselected. Booking a visit alone does not create a repair or approve a quote.

1. The assigned luthier opens the intervention and selects **Record instrument intake**, recording its condition, accessories and requested work when it arrives.
2. The luthier sends a quote with the total including applicable taxes, currency, scope, parts, timing and terms. Supported currencies are EUR, USD, GBP, CAD and CHF. Payment is arranged directly with the workshop.
3. The instrument owner reviews the latest quote and chooses **Approve this quote** or **Decline this quote**. Before approval, the luthier can revise it; every version remains in **Workshop activity**. An older form cannot approve a newer quote.
4. After approval, the luthier selects **Start work** and records progress. An approved quote stays in the history. Use **Propose additional work** if a discovery requires a different agreement.
5. The luthier records collection instructions and selects **Notify owner: ready for collection**. The owner receives an in-app notification linking to the intervention.
6. Once the instrument is handed back, the luthier selects **Record collection**. The intervention list shows the workshop stage. Signing a report and recording collection are separate actions.

Before approval, either participant can cancel the work with a reason and return arrangements. After approval, the luthier records any cancellation. Cancellation closes the job and retains its activity; it does not confirm that an instrument was physically returned. A closed job cannot be reopened. Create a new intervention for later work.

An instrument with an active workshop job cannot be transferred, even when a report has already been signed. Collection or cancellation closes the job. Intake notes, quotes, approvals and progress are private to the original requester and assigned workshop; they do not follow the instrument to a buyer. Record the work performed in a signed report below the workshop activity so it becomes part of the instrument’s service history. Leave prices, personal details and private collection arrangements out of that report.


## Approve additional work during a repair

When a luthier discovers a problem during work, **Propose additional work** pauses the repair. The proposal must explain the discovery, describe the complete revised scope (including the existing work), give a replacement total including applicable taxes in the same currency, and set an estimated completion date. The owner sees the current agreement, revised total and difference side by side. This is a new total, not an additional charge to add again.

Only the owner can choose **Approve revised agreement and resume work** or **Decline this change**. Approval replaces the current agreement and resumes work; earlier quotes, approvals and proposals remain in **Workshop activity**. If the luthier revises a pending proposal, reopen the page before approving it. If its estimated completion date has passed, ask the luthier for an updated proposal.

Refusal keeps the current agreement unchanged and leaves the repair paused. The luthier can send another proposal, cancel with return arrangements, or explain why the original scope can safely continue and choose **Resume existing approved work only**. Resuming withdraws the proposal; it does not authorise additional work. Work cannot be marked ready or collected while paused. The same private-access and transfer restrictions apply to change requests as to the original repair.
