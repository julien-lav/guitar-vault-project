# Beta participant tasks

Use only the fictitious accounts and files supplied by the facilitator. Choose English in the app. Add the agreed session label to your test instrument and file titles so you can recognise them. Say what you expect to happen before sharing anything. You may use **Help & guides** at any point; tell the facilitator if a guide leaves you unsure.

## Owner — phase 1

Your goal is to prepare an instrument for an authenticity assessment.

1. Find your collection and add an instrument with the session label. Give it a serial number, a private cost note and the supplied photo.
2. Add a private invoice and a certificate from the supplied files. Explain who you think can read each one now.
3. Find the luthier’s workshop after they tell you it is ready. Ask them to assess this instrument’s authenticity. Put a fictitious appointment detail in the request that you would not want to show a future owner.
4. Tell the facilitator when the request is ready. Wait for the luthier to finish phase 2.

## Luthier — prepare your workshop, then phase 2

Your goal is to provide a professional opinion while keeping your own financial document private.

1. Find your workshop page. Create or update its fictitious presentation and make it discoverable. Tell the owner its name. Explain what its identity-verification status means to you.
2. Once the owner has sent the request, find it and read the appointment detail.
3. Record a report with findings, evidence and limitations. Treat this as fictional test work, not a real authenticity assessment.
4. Add your supplied workshop invoice so only you can read it. Add the inspection file so current and future owners can read it with the report.
5. Record a correction with a reason. Find both revisions and explain which is current.
6. Tell the owner when the work is complete.

## Owner — phase 3

Your goal is to hand over the instrument while retaining your private information.

1. Find the report and its correction. Explain which parts you expect the buyer to receive.
2. Propose a transfer to the buyer’s exact username supplied by the facilitator. Include the certificate, keeping your invoice private.
3. Explain whether the buyer can download the certificate before accepting. Ask the buyer to start phase 4.

## Buyer — phase 4

Your goal is to receive the passport and check what has actually been shared.

1. Find the proposal. Before accepting, look for the available instrument details and document information. Describe what is still unavailable.
2. Accept the proposal. Find the instrument in your collection, both report revisions, the inspection evidence and the certificate. Download the certificate.
3. Try to find the former owner’s private cost note, original photo, invoice and appointment detail. Tell the facilitator what is visible; do not assume something is private just because you did not find it immediately.
4. Give the certificate a personal title, archive it, find it again and restore it. Check that you can still download it.

## Owner and buyer — phase 5

Your goal is to check the boundaries of the handover.

- Owner: find your original invoice, private photo and archived notes. Check that the buyer’s personal certificate title did not change your own title.
- Buyer: add an unused test document, change its category and delete it. Describe the confirmation and check that it no longer appears. Compare this with the actions available for the received certificate.
- Each person: find a Help answer about a privacy question without the facilitator naming the guide. Try reading it on a small screen and switch to French and back.

## Finish

Tell the facilitator: What was hardest to find? Which sharing choice felt uncertain? Did the guides answer your questions? What would you change first?
